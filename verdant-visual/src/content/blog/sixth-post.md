Every developer knows the oldest fix in IT: turn it off and on again. Nvidia seems to have discovered the enterprise edition. Within days, Jensen Huang resigned as CEO, admitted an affair in a memo to staff, and, according to the Financial Times, started discussing a comeback with his allies. That's faster than most companies approve a pull request.

*An ironic take based on public reporting as of November 15, 2026. Several claims are still under investigation, and nothing below goes beyond what the linked outlets reported.*

## The Changelog
**Breaking changes shipped. Release notes pending.**

- **October:** The New York Times reported that Huang had developed a "close relationship" with a former tech executive whom US officials believe may have been passing information to China. China has denied the espionage claims, and the US Justice Department hasn't reached a conclusion.
- **Last week:** Nvidia's board opened an investigation into whether Huang was the "target of a foreign intelligence effort." He resigned, and on the same day admitted an extramarital affair in a memo to staff.
- **This weekend:** The FT reported that Huang has already discussed a return with allies.

In software we'd call that a hotfix, a rollback, and a revert of the rollback, all before anyone has written the post-mortem.

## Bus Factor: One Leather Jacket
**The AI economy runs on GPUs. Nvidia, it turns out, ran on one person.**

Engineers measure the *bus factor*: how many people would have to disappear before a project stalls. For one of the most valuable companies on the planet, the answer turned out to be one, and nobody even needed a bus.

Huang's much-quoted line is that you won't lose your job to AI, but to someone who uses AI. There was a third option nobody put on the slide: a board investigation, a resignation, and a memo admitting an affair, all on the same day.

Even the Guardian treated the story as a cautionary tale for family businesses, filed, beautifully, under *US small business*.

**Source:** [Guardian (2026)](https://www.theguardian.com/business/2026/nov/15/nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-affair-business)

## The Treasury Secretary Will See You Now
**Silicon Valley asked for less government. It got a headhunter.**

US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent went on Fox's *Sunday Morning Futures* to advise Nvidia's board to look for candidates "outside the company," because "they need a dramatic shake-up." He also wants answers on export-control compliance within ten days.

"Nvidia is a wonderful company. Jensen Huang is an incredible innovator, but that does not abrogate their duties to compliance," he said, which may be the most expensive job reference ever given on Sunday morning TV.

Meanwhile, a growing number of people in tech believe the spy allegations might have been overblown by China hawks who wanted Huang out; he had argued for selling chips to China. Export controls, it seems, now come with a personnel section.

**Source:** [Business Insider (2026)](https://www.businessinsider.com/scott-bessent-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-replacement-search-2026-11)

## #BringJensenBack: The Sequel
**November is apparently CEO-reboot season in AI.**

Elon Musk and Epic Games CEO Tim Sweeney are among the signatories of an open letter asking the board to "Bring Jensen Back," several Nvidia employees are backing their former boss, and, according to the FT, a group of influential executives is setting up a "war room" to get him reinstated.

If this feels familiar, you've seen the pilot. In November 2023, OpenAI's board fired Sam Altman, most of the staff threatened to quit, and he was back within days. Same month, new season, bigger GPUs.

**Source:** [Bild, citing the FT (2026)](https://www.bild.de/politik/ausland-und-internationales/financial-times-meldet-huang-spricht-schon-ueber-rueckkehr-als-nvidia-chef-6afa07189849eea0701e3ea4)

## The Interim Patch
**When in doubt, give root access to the CFO.**

While the comeback campaign runs, CFO Colette Kress is serving as interim CEO, with quarterly results due on Tuesday. That's a brave week to inherit the keynote. The board says it will consider internal and external candidates, and the FT reported that it has already contacted Arm CEO René Haas.

One detail spoils the movie ending: unlike some founder-CEOs, Huang doesn't hold a controlling stake, so he can't simply overrule the board. Founder mode, meet permission denied.

**Source:** [Business Insider (2026)](https://www.businessinsider.com/scott-bessent-nvidia-ceo-jensen-huang-replacement-search-2026-11), [Bild (2026)](https://www.bild.de/politik/ausland-und-internationales/financial-times-meldet-huang-spricht-schon-ueber-rueckkehr-als-nvidia-chef-6afa07189849eea0701e3ea4)

## Lessons for the Rest of Us

**Succession Is Not a Nice-to-Have:** If your company can't survive one person stepping away, you don't have a company, you have a dependency. Write the runbook before you need it.

**Governance Beats Charisma:** Boards exist for the weeks when the keynote is cancelled, not for the quarters when the stock goes up.

**Rollbacks Aren't Free:** Reverting a release means admitting the release was the problem. Reinstating a CEO days after he resigned would be the boldest rollback in corporate history, and someone would still have to write the post-mortem.

**The Real Question:** Nvidia sells the hardware that's supposed to make so much human work optional. Can it find the one human it actually needs?
