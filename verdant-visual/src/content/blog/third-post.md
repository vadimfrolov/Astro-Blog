## Air Canada's Chatbot Creates Fake Policy
**Chatbot invents a discount, tribunal makes airline pay for its fiction.**

Air Canada's AI chatbot incorrectly promised a grieving customer bereavement fare discounts. When the promised refund never came, a Canadian tribunal ruled against the airline, ordering them to honor the nonexistent policy and pay CAD$1,000 in damages. The company's argument that "the chatbot was responsible" was rejected—businesses bear full responsibility for their AI agents.

**Source:** [Guardian (2024)](https://www.theguardian.com/world/2024/feb/16/air-canada-chatbot-lawsuit)

## McDonald's Scraps AI Drive-Thru After Order Chaos
**Misheard orders pile on McNuggets—and pile drive the pilot program.**

McDonald's terminated its AI drive-thru ordering program after embarrassing failures went viral. The system frequently misheard customers, adding "hundreds of dollars of McNuggets" or random items like butter packets to sundaes. Facing customer frustration and brand damage, McDonald's pulled the plug by July 2024, admitting the tech needed more work.

**Source:** [Guardian (2024)](https://www.theguardian.com/business/article/2024/jun/17/mcdonalds-ends-ai-drive-thru)

## AI Tenant Screening Settles $2.2M Bias Lawsuit
**Algorithm red-lines voucher holders, ends in costly reckoning.**

SafeRent's AI tenant scoring systematically discriminated against Black and Hispanic renters with housing vouchers. Despite steady payment histories, the algorithm gave failing scores, causing automatic rejections. The company settled for $2.2 million and must stop offering accept/decline scores for voucher holders—one of the first major AI bias settlements.

**Source:** [Guardian (2024)](https://www.theguardian.com/technology/2024/dec/14/saferent-ai-tenant-screening-lawsuit)

## IBM Watson's $4B Healthcare Failure
**$4B later, "Dr. Watson" misdiagnoses its own market viability.**

IBM poured $4 billion into Watson for Oncology, promising revolutionary cancer care. Instead, it gave "unsafe or incorrect" treatment advice, struggled with hospital variations, and suggested inappropriate therapies. Major clients like MD Anderson scrapped their Watson projects. IBM discontinued the program in 2023, selling Watson Health for a fraction of its investment.

**Source:** [Henrico Dolfing (2024)](https://www.henricodolfing.com/2024/12/case-study-ibm-watson-for-oncology-failure.html)

## Samsung Bans ChatGPT After Data Leak
**Engineers paste secrets, company pastes AI ban.**

Samsung engineers accidentally uploaded confidential source code and meeting notes to ChatGPT, handing trade secrets to OpenAI's servers. Unable to retrieve the data, Samsung banned ChatGPT company-wide in May 2023. The incident triggered similar restrictions across industries as companies realized the IP risks of public AI tools.

**Source:** [TechCrunch (2023)](https://techcrunch.com/2023/05/02/samsung-bans-use-of-generative-ai-tools-like-chatgpt-after-april-internal-data-leak/)

## Warsaw Stock Exchange Algorithm Meltdown
**HFT algorithms spiral, forcing one-hour trading halt.**

Automated trading algorithms flooded Poland's Warsaw Stock Exchange with orders, causing extreme volatility. The exchange suspended all trading for 75 minutes—unprecedented in recent years—to prevent market meltdown. The WIG20 index had plunged 7% before the emergency halt, highlighting systemic risks of unchecked algorithmic trading.

**Source:** [Bloomberg (2025)](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2025-04-10/warsaw-bourse-reviews-algo-rules-after-trading-halt-hits-brokers)

## Car Dealership Chatbot Sells $75K SUV for $1
**Prompt-engineered prank discounts into oblivion.**

Pranksters used prompt engineering to manipulate a Chevrolet dealership's GPT-4 chatbot into "agreeing" to sell a $58,000 Tahoe for $1, claiming it was "legally binding—no takesies backsies." The viral stunt forced the dealership to shut down the AI assistant and highlighted how users can manipulate customer-facing chatbots to produce absurd, business-undermining outputs.

**Source:** [Cut The SaaS (2023)](https://www.cut-the-saas.com/ai/chatbot-case-study-purchasing-a-chevrolet-tahoe-for-dollar-1)

## The Hidden Costs of AI Magic

**Dependency Risk:** Using third-party AI tools makes you vulnerable. Prices can spike anytime, and your stability depends on providers' stability. Remember when Amazon's US-EAST-1 failure paralyzed companies for hours?

**Success Story Bias:** Every success story hides countless failures that never get public attention. Not every workflow needs complex AI—traditional automation often delivers equal value at lower cost and risk.

**The Real Question:** Does the magic trick justify the price when it goes wrong?
